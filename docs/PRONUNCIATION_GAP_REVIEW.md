# Review of seven HKCanCor pronunciation gaps

These seven missing **single-character pairs** are not seven approved dictionary additions. Three have attested word-level or contraction contexts; one is explicitly discussed as a pronunciation contrast; two raise written-character questions; one has conflicting lexicographic tone evidence. **All seven remain pending human review.** No dictionary, frozen evaluation label, score or frozen `data/review-v2` file was changed.

The separate [evidence pack](../data/pronunciation-gap-review/review.json) preserves the 19 occurrences across the selected 500 cases, original tokens and source coordinates, raw retained table rows, source hashes, short primary-source excerpts and blank reviewer fields. Full-corpus occurrence counts below are separately counted across the 58 pinned transcripts with one-character/one-syllable source alignment; they are not counts of independent speakers or recordings.

## Evidence and remaining questions

**哩 `ni1`.** Oi Yee Kwong's PACLIC 2018 paper, printed page 313 footnote 1, explicitly recognizes 呢/哩 spellings and the variants `li1`, `lei1`, `ni1`, `nei1`. It also uses HKCanCor and discusses its transcription convention, so this is an independent research statement, **not independent recording validation** of these 973 tokens. The retained Rime words file already supplies 哩個 `ni1 go3`; retained LSHK single-character rows give `li1`, `le1`, `lei5`. Review should concern the demonstrative and its written variants, preserving other 哩 senses. [Kwong 2018](https://aclanthology.org/Y18-1036.pdf), [pinned LSHK table](https://github.com/lshk-org/jyutping-table/blob/dad2dd6d6f02fc51138ecc6818f7b38eba5c2ad3/list.tsv).

**窿 `lung2`.** The sampled token is `頂窿/d/ding2lung2/`, FC-108a_v2 turn 121. The other full-source occurrence is also 頂窿. The retained official words.hk wordlist independently lists **頂窿, 頂櫳 and 頂籠**, all `ding2 lung2`; Rime already has 頂窿 `ding2 lung2`. CUHK's character reference lists ordinary `lung1`/`lung4` contexts. The lexical reading is attested; these checks do not establish a general `lung2` character entry or a particular historical changed-tone mechanism. [Official wordlist and declaration](https://words.hk/faiman/analysis/wordslist/), [CUHK 窿](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=窿).

**肆 `si5`.** Both corpus tokens are 食肆 in FC-R004_v2. LSHK, the retained words.hk wordlist and the current words.hk 食肆 headword give `si3`. However, the primary words.hk **杜絕 usage example explicitly annotates 食肆 as `sik6 si5`**. That contradicts a blanket claim that `si5` is unattested outside HKCanCor. The checked references do not explain the discrepancy as changed tone. Distinguishing contextual speech from dictionary-example or corpus annotation error still needs linguistic review. [CUHK si table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s1=s&s2=i), [words.hk 食肆](https://words.hk/zidin/食肆), [words.hk 杜絕](https://words.hk/zidin/杜絕).

**沸 `fat6`.** FC-R016_v turns 36–44 contain a pronunciation discussion: turn 37 juxtaposes `鼎沸/a/ding2fat6/` and `鼎沸/a/ding2fai3/`; subsequent turns discuss 沸, 沸點 and 頂心頂肺. The selected turn 43 belongs to this exchange. CUHK gives `fai3` and includes 人聲鼎沸. The strongest conclusion is a **metalinguistic contrast in the transcript**, not a licensed ordinary `fat6` reading. No audio was inspected and no speaker's pronunciation was corrected. [Original source](https://github.com/fcbond/hkcancor/blob/39aeadf920e0b5ca93d0ad7792c59e740e7bdd65/data/utf8/FC-R016_v), [CUHK 沸](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=沸), [CUHK fai table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s1=f&s2=ai).

**蚚 `sik1`.** All three corpus occurrences are 蚚蜴 in FC-045_v2. Retained words.hk has 蜥蜴 `sik1 jik6`; LSHK/CUHK distinguish 蚚 `kei4` from 蜥 `sik1`. Taiwan's Ministry of Education entry for 蚚 describes a rice insect; it supplies character/meaning evidence, not Cantonese pronunciation evidence. A written-character substitution is plausible, but no checked primary reference establishes 蚚 as an accepted variant of 蜥. Preserve 蚚 and review the original transcription intention. [CUHK kei table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s1=k&s2=ei), [CUHK sik table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s1=s&s2=ik), [MOE 蚚](https://dict.variants.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=69756&la=1).

**跤 `gaau3`.** FC-R011_v turn 1 contains both `脫骹/v/tyut3gaau3/` and `脫跤/v/tyut3gaau3/` in the same injury narrative, with 膊頭 between them. CUHK/LSHK list 跤 `gaau1` (LSHK also `haau1`); these do not attest `gaau3` for 跤. The source itself supplies an orthographic inconsistency. That does not establish which spelling to prefer, or justify a general tone rule. [Original source](https://github.com/fcbond/hkcancor/blob/39aeadf920e0b5ca93d0ad7792c59e740e7bdd65/data/utf8/FC-R011_v), [CUHK gaau table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-can/pho-rel.php?s1=g&s2=aau).

**三 `saa1`.** The sampled `三𠻺幾/m/saa1aa6gei2/` occurs in a temperature discussion; the other occurrence is 三𠻺 before 歲. The primary words.hk 卅 entry explicitly describes the thirty contraction and gives `saa1 aa6`/`saa1 a6`, including 卅幾. CUHK distinguishes 卅 `saa1` from 三 `saam1`/`saam3`. Retained Rime entries such as 年三十晚 already encode `nin4 saa1 aa6 maan5`. This calls for word-level contraction/transcription review; mechanically aligned characters are not necessarily independent pronunciation units. [words.hk 卅](https://words.hk/zidin/卅), [CUHK 卅](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=卅), [CUHK 三](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=三).
